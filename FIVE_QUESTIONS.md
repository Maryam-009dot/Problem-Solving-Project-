Five Questions
1. What is a row?
In the raw WDI export, one row is one series for one country. The Data sheet had 304 rows= 299 of them are real and the remaining 5 are blank or footer lines. After cleaning, one row is one country-year, produced 506 rows and 16 columns, which equals 22 countries x 23 years. Confidence: high, because the counts match exactly and I checked for duplicate country-series pairs and found 0.

2. What is the unit of the outcome?
The outcome is under-5 mortality. The metadata defines it as "the probability per 1,000 that a newborn baby will die" before age five, given that year's age-specific mortality rates. So it is a probability expressed per 1,000, not a raw count of deaths in that year. Observed values range from 5.9 to 177.3, which looks plausible. Confidence: high for the definition, because I read it in the Series - Metadata sheet

3. What is the timeframe?

The raw file covers 2000 to 2024, but my analysis uses 2000 to 2022. I dropped 2023 and 2024 because current health expenditure is missing for all 23 countries in 2024, and skilled birth attendance is missing for all 23 countries in 2023 and 2024. After cleaning, every one of the 22 countries has all 23 years. Confidence: high for coverage; medium for the cutoff choice, because it is a judgement call that removes the most recent years.

4. Who is missing?
I printed the unique country names and found 23 real countries. By design the data is limited to Pakistan and 22 chosen peers, so every other country is excluded.I considered the choosen peers countries as comparable to Pakistan in development conditions and that no formal selection criterion was used. Only 6 of the 22 peers are South Asian (Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Nepal, Sri Lanka); the rest are from Africa, South-East Asia, Central Asia and the Middle East. I removed Yemen because its GDP per capita is missing for all 25 years, while the other 22 countries have complete GDP. Missing values are concentrated in some countries rather than spread randomly, for female primary completion, Afghanistan and Kenya are missing 20 of 25 years, Bangladesh, Nigeria and Tajikistan 17, whereas Cambodia and Uzbekistan are missing only 1. After cleaning, skilled birth attendance is missing and female primary completion in 182. A national figure also hides differences inside Pakistan, such as provinces and rural versus urban areas, and this dataset cannot show them. I could not find out from the metadata how complete vital registration is in Pakistan, so I treat that as unknown. Confidence: high for the named countries and counts, low for who is hidden inside the national averages.
5. What was the sampling frame?
These are not survey results. The under-5 mortality figure in WDI is a modelled estimate from the UN Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation. According to the metadata, the model combines vital registration, surveys and censuses, and fits a country-specific regression to produce a best-estimate trend line. The metadata also warns that complete vital registration is uncommon in developing countries, so many estimates rely on surveys or indirect techniques. Because the series is a fitted trend, it is smooth over time, and the years within one country are strongly related, so my effective sample is small. The set of countries is a purposive, non-probability sample that I chose myself.
